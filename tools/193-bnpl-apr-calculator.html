Methodology, Source Notes & Regulatory Citations
  • CCD2 Directive 2023/2225/EU, Art. 31 — APR calculation using actuarial method. The APR is the rate equating the present value of all drawdowns to the present value of all repayments and charges. Formula: Σ Ck/(1+i)^tk = Σ Dk/(1+i)^sk, where i is the APR (expressed as a decimal divided by 100), and t/s are time periods in years. CCD2 Annex I provides the full mathematical specification. APR is an effective annual rate (not nominal).
  • TILA Regulation Z, Appendix J — Annual Percentage Rate computations for closed-end credit. The US method uses a unit-period approach. The APR is the nominal rate obtained by multiplying the unit-period rate by the number of unit-periods in a year. For monthly payments: APR = monthly_rate × 12 (nominal annual). This differs from CCD2: CCD2 gives an effective annual rate ((1+r)^12 - 1), while Reg Z gives a nominal rate (r × 12).
  • Zero-interest BNPL: when there are no fees and interest rate = 0%, APR = 0% under both CCD2 and Reg Z, regardless of payment timing. However, if a mandatory origination fee or administration charge is bundled into the BNPL arrangement, CCD2 Art. 31 requires that charge to be included in the APR calculation, which may produce a non-zero APR even for "0% interest" products.
  • APR divergence: common BNPL marketing risk. If a merchant advertises "0% APR" but the BNPL arrangement includes mandatory fees, the regulatory APR may differ. Similarly, deferred first payment changes the present value calculation. CFPB Supervisory Highlights 2023 and FCA CP23/5 cite APR misrepresentation as a specific enforcement risk in BNPL marketing.
  • Newton-Raphson iterative solver: the actuarial APR equation is solved numerically using the Newton-Raphson method (up to 200 iterations, convergence threshold 1×10⁻⁸). For zero-interest, zero-fee products, the solver returns 0% directly without iteration. For equal-instalment loans without fees, the annuity formula provides an exact closed-form solution that the tool uses as the initial guess. Client-side only. Zero PII.
