Readiness Sponsor Bank

Card Programme Launch Readiness Checker

Start here to establish your baseline before investing in scheme enrolment fees or compliance work. This tool scores your programme across six dimensions — product structure, BIN sponsor health, regulatory registration, programme controls, technical readiness, and go-to-market. It surfaces gaps that must be resolved before Visa or Mastercard will accept your programme application.

↳ Inputs
Programme structure, product type (debit / prepaid / credit), BIN sponsor identity, target geographies, regulatory registrations, technical readiness self-assessment.
→ Outputs to Step 2
Readiness score (0–100), gap priority list, product type confirmation and MCC candidates — feed into the interchange qualification tester.

T225
Interchange Visa / Mastercard

Visa / Mastercard Interchange Qualification Tester

Interchange yield determines whether your card programme unit economics are viable. This tool tests your MCC codes, average ticket sizes, card product type, and acceptance environment against current Visa and Mastercard interchange qualification criteria. Run this before building a P&L model — a misclassified MCC or wrong card tier can swing yield by 80–150 bps.

↳ Inputs from Step 1
MCC candidates from Step 1, card product type (debit / credit / prepaid), average ticket size, acceptance channel (card-present / CNP / e-commerce), transaction volume estimates.
→ Outputs to Step 3
Interchange qualification tier matrix, estimated yield by product type, acceptance environment flags — informs the PCI scope determination in Step 3.

T226
PCI DSS v4.0 Scope Wizard

PCI DSS v4.0 Scope Assessment Wizard

PCI DSS scope determines your SAQ type and the volume of compliance work ahead. This wizard maps your card data flows, tokenisation approach, and acceptance methods to a cardholder data environment (CDE) boundary, then determines whether you qualify for SAQ A, SAQ A-EP, SAQ D, or a full QSA-led Report on Compliance. Scoping errors made here cascade into audit findings later.

↳ Inputs from Step 2
Acceptance channels from Step 2 (e-commerce vs. card-present vs. CNP), tokenisation status, payment processor relationship, card data storage approach.
→ Outputs to Step 4
CDE boundary definition, SAQ type determination, controls checklist — the e-commerce acceptance flag flows into 3DS applicability analysis in Step 4.

04
T228
3DS 2.x EMV / PSD2

3DS 2.x / EMV 3D Secure Compliance Checker

EMV 3D Secure (3DS 2.x) is mandatory for e-commerce liability shift under Visa Secure and Mastercard Identity Check, and forms the backbone of Strong Customer Authentication (SCA) under PSD2 in Europe. This checker maps your merchant categories and integration approach to 3DS obligations, identifies SCA exemption opportunities (low-value, trusted beneficiary, TRA), and flags liability exposure for transactions that bypass authentication.

↳ Inputs from Step 3
E-commerce acceptance flag from Step 3, merchant category codes, ACS integration approach, geography (EEA for PSD2 / SCA scope), transaction risk assessment (TRA) capability.
→ Outputs to Step 5
3DS compliance checklist, liability exposure map, SCA exemption eligibility — authentication pass-rates feed directly into scheme fee modelling in Step 5.

T233
Fee Model Benchmarks

Card Scheme Fee Benchmarking Tool

The final step closes the economics loop. Plug in your volume projections, BIN sponsor relationship, network (Visa / Mastercard / dual), and geography to benchmark your all-in scheme fee cost per transaction against market reference ranges. This confirms whether your interchange yield from Step 2 is sufficient to cover scheme fees and produce a viable net economics before you commit to launch costs.

↳ Inputs from Steps 1–4
Volume projections, BIN sponsor tier, network selection, product type, geography, authentication pass-rate from Step 4, interchange yield estimate from Step 2.
→ Final Output
Scheme fee benchmarking table, cost-per-transaction estimate, all-in net economics summary — ready for the business case and launch paperwork.
