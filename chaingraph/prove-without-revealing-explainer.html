Prove without revealing

A manufacturer holds a signed record of one shipment. It shares the record with the supplier's name and the price elided, the buyer still checks it, and when a dispute arises the manufacturer re-reveals one elided field to an Reviewer alone. This is a commitment to a record, verifiable after parts are elided. The walkthrough is canned: every value on it is an illustrative teaching value, labelled as such, and every hash is shown visibly truncated, like a3f9….

Goal of this walkthroughShow the four-party selective disclosure story end to end: sign, elide, share, verify, dispute, re-reveal to one party, and why salt matters for an elided name drawn from a short list. Nothing here runs a server, several devices or a conformant implementation; the hands-on version lives in the elision comparison workbench.
Where the values come fromAll names, prices and hashes on this page are illustrative, made up for teaching, and never recorded from a real run. The digest-tree drawing illustrates the digest rules of the Gordian Envelope draft, draft-mcnally-envelope, which is beta; the Blockchain Commons implementation of it is also beta, is not audited, and is not used by the AINumbers estate today.

Step 1 of 8

Four parties and one shipment

A supplier sells a lot of steel to a manufacturer, which ships finished parts to a buyer. An Reviewer stands outside the deal in case the two later disagree. The manufacturer holds a signed record of the shipment, and every name and number in it is synthetic.

The record is ordinary commercial paper: who supplied the steel, what was paid, what was shipped and of what grade. Its only special property is how it can be shared later. The signature at the top of the record covers the whole of it, which is what lets parts disappear without breaking the check.

2
Step 2 of 8

The record becomes a digest tree

Each field of the record is hashed, pairs of hashes are hashed together, and one root digest stands for the whole record. The manufacturer's signature covers the root. Change any leaf and the root changes; the signature then fails against the root it was made on.

This tree shape follows the digest rules described in draft-mcnally-envelope: every field and every combination is digested, so any part of the record can be named by its own hash. Every hash printed on this page is a teaching value and is truncated on purpose; a full fake digest would invite someone to check it against something real.

3
Step 3 of 8

Elide the supplier's name and the price

Before sharing, the manufacturer removes two leaves: the supplier's name and the price. What it sends is not a damaged record but a shorter one: the surviving leaves, plus the two digests of the removed leaves in their places. An elided node keeps its digest.

The buyer receives weight and grade in the clear and two opaque digests where the secrets were. Nothing was rewritten: the shared copy walks up to the same root digest the signature was made on. The copy is a commitment to a record, verifiable after parts are elided.

4
Step 4 of 8

The buyer checks it without the secrets

The buyer recomputes the tree from what it received and checks the signature against the root. Both checks pass. The buyer has now confirmed the shipment record is exactly the one the manufacturer signed, without ever seeing who supplied it or what was paid.

This is the everyday state of the deal: the buyer can hold the manufacturer to the record, and the supplier relationship stays private. If the manufacturer later handed over a different weight or grade, the recomputed root would move away from the signed one and the check would fail loudly.

5
Step 5 of 8

A dispute arises

Months later the buyer claims the shipment does not match its contract and names the wrong supplier in a claim. The manufacturer wants to settle it in front of an Reviewer, and it can do that without publishing the price to the buyer or the world.

The dispute is about a fact the shared copy genuinely contains but hides. Nothing needs re-signing and nothing needs re-sending: the record already commits to the supplier's name through the elided leaf digest b7c2…91aa. What is missing is a way to show one more field to exactly one person, which is the next step.

6
Step 6 of 8

One field re-revealed to the Reviewer only

The manufacturer hands the supplier's name to the Reviewer alone. The Reviewer hashes it, finds b7c2…91aa, the exact digest in the shared copy, and the root is still the signed a3f9…c07e. The claim collapses for the Reviewer, and the buyer learns nothing it did not already have.

Re-revealing is cheap because the commitment never moved. The revealed value does not get pasted into the record; it is checked against the digest that was there all along. The Reviewer now knows the name, the price leaf stays elided, and the same move would work later for the price if the dispute ever reached it.

7
Step 7 of 8

Why salt matters for an elided name

The elided leaf is only as private as its input is hard to guess. Steel suppliers number in the dozens, so anyone holding the unsalted digest of the supplier's name can hash each candidate in turn and match it in a few steps. A salted commitment over the same name defeats that list.

This is the same lesson the estate's salted commitment scheme teaches: enumerability is the test, not the presence of a digest. Any elision design over low-entropy fields, supplier names, country codes, price brackets, needs a fresh secret salt per value, or the elision is a light curtain rather than a wall. Try the attack yourself in the salted hash enumerability demo.

8
Step 8 of 8

In the AINumbers estate

The same shipment record could be shared three ways with estate mechanisms. Two of them exist today; the digest tree of this walkthrough is outside the estate and stays a teaching drawing.

MechanismHow it hides the fieldStatus
SD-JWT disclosuresThe issuer signs the full record; each hidden field is carried as a separate salted disclosure the holder may omit, and the verifier checks each shown disclosure against the signed disclosure hashes.the estate does this today try T545
sha256-salted@1 private inputsA workflow input is replaced by its salted commitment before a run, so the recorded artifact never carries the raw value at all.the estate does this today try the demo
Gordian Envelope elisionThe digest tree of steps 2 to 6: elided nodes keep their digests and the root still verifies.outside the estate hands-on workbench

The estate's core integrity path is the JCS execution_hash: an artifact's hash is recomputed from its full canonical bytes, so eliding parts of an execution_hash artifact, or of a Policy Mandate export, is parked by design; the preimage is untouchable and a partial artifact would no longer recompute. Envelope elision is a different format for a different job, and this page illustrates the draft's digest rules without claiming conformance or interop.

  • One record, one signature, one root digest across every step of the walkthrough.
  • Elided nodes keep their digests, so a re-revealed field checks against the shared copy without re-signing.
  • An unsalted elided digest over a short supplier list is guessable in a few steps; salt is what closes that.
  • Live estate surfaces for the same idea: T545 for SD-JWT disclosures and the salted-hash demo for sha256-salted@1.
