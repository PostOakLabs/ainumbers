Dependent Care FSA vs. CDCTC Optimizer

Maximise childcare tax savings across three strategies: a Dependent Care FSA alone, the Child & Dependent Care Tax Credit alone, or the optimal combination. FICA savings on the FSA contribution are included, exactly as the ported source models them.

All inputs are processed locally in your browser. Nothing is transmitted, stored, or logged. Inputs disappear when you close the tab.
2026 rules: the DC-FSA exclusion cap is $7,500 ($3,750 on a separate return by a married individual); IRC §129(a)(2)(A) as amended by Pub. L. 119-21 §70404, effective for taxable years beginning after 12/31/2025. The CDCTC applies a percentage to up to $3,000 ($6,000 for 2+ qualifying individuals) of expenses not offset by the FSA; IRC §21(c)(2), the limit reduced by the IRC §129 exclusion. For 2026 the applicable percentage starts at 50%, falls 1 point per $2,000 of AGI over $15,000 to a 35% floor, then falls 1 point per $2,000 ($4,000 joint) over $75,000 ($150,000 joint) to a 20% floor; IRC §21(a)(2) as amended by Pub. L. 119-21 §70405. FICA savings on the contribution are the 7.65% employee share (IRC §3101(a) 6.2% + §3101(b)(1) 1.45%). Sources: IRC §129; IRC §21; IRS Pub. 503; Pub. L. 119-21 §§70404-70405.
