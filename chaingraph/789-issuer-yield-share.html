Who keeps the reserve income and who pays the yield?

White-label stablecoin issuance splits reserve income between the issuer, the platform and the distributors. This page walks through how node 789-issuer-yield-share recomputes that split from aggregate declared values, traces every holder-facing reward back to its payer and its funding source, and flags the one flow the enacted US federal statute prohibits: an issuer paying interest or yield to the coin's holder. Every number below is synthetic; the node reads declared values only and never reaches a chain, a bank or a real agreement.

Step 1

The deal and its flows

In a white-label arrangement a brand issues a payment stablecoin on infrastructure run by a platform. The coin's reserves earn income; a commercial agreement divides that income. Some arrangements also push rewards to the people who hold the coin.

The node takes the deal as four declared blocks: a profile (the enacted statutory regime), a period (days plus either a declared reserve income or an average reserve value with an annual yield in basis points), a split (per-party shares in basis points or fixed minor-unit amounts), and a list of holder rewards, each carrying a payer role, a funding source, a value and a conditioning. It reads role labels (issuer, platform, distributor, custodian), never legal names, and integer minor units, never floats.

Step 2

Recompute the income, then the split

If the deal declares a reserve income, that figure is the base. Otherwise the node accrues it: annual yield in basis points applied linearly over the period's days to the declared average reserve value. Shares follow, and one rounding rule governs everything: floor to integer minor units.

Conservation is the integrity check that catches an agreement that silently over-allocates: if the parties' shares add up to more or less than the income the reserves actually produced, the numbers do not describe the same money. A basis-point split must also name the full 10,000 bp. The node reports the allocated sum alongside the income so a reviewer can see the size of any gap rather than a bare boolean.

Step 3

Trace every holder reward

A holder sees a reward and reasonably asks two different questions: who paid it, and what money funded it. The answers can diverge: a distributor can write the cheque from the issuer's share of reserve income, and that distinction is exactly what the enacted statute turns on.

Each reward's trace row records the payer role, the funding source, the value, the conditioning, and two booleans: payer_is_issuer and funded_by_issuer_flow. Conditioning (holding, activity, none) is carried as a declared fact. The rows are pinned to the paragraph that the enacted profile implements, so the output carries its own citation.

Step 4

Verdicts, flags and the worked vectors

Three verdicts, driven only by the trace: an issuer-paid reward yields ISSUER_YIELD_PRESENT; an issuer-funded reward paid by somebody else yields REVIEW; everything else is NO_ISSUER_YIELD. Split defects flag independently. The enacted profile is pinned on every output; the node ships no proposed-legislation profile because no settled bill text was pinnable at build.

All six vectors are synthetic: every value is invented and no real deal terms or named counterparties appear anywhere in the node. A seventh vector refuses an unknown profile: the node ships exactly one profile, the enacted statutory one, and refuses anything else rather than guessing at a regime it has no pinned text for. The statutory prohibition this node implements, with its pinned enrolled text and hash, is carried in the node's cited-clause record: Public Law 119-27 § 4(a)(11), which bars a permitted payment stablecoin issuer from paying a holder any form of interest or yield solely in connection with the holding, use or retention of the coin.

    Step 5

    Limits and adjacency

    The node states facts about declared flows. It does not read a chain, a bank statement or a contract, and it does not say that any deal is lawful, compliant or enforceable. A REVIEW verdict means the declared inputs cannot resolve the statutory question, so the trace goes to a professional.

    What this node is

    An aggregate recompute: income, per-party shares with conservation, a per-reward payer-and-funding trace, three verdicts, and flags. Every output carries the enacted profile label and the pinned paragraph.

    What this node is not

    It carries no legal opinion, holds no registry of real deals, and reads no chain. It never sees addresses, names or live terms, only the declared aggregates you paste.

    Adjacency: the narrow sibling is the genius_yield check inside the Arc xReserve linter (art-45), which flags a declared yield switch on a configuration, whereas this node recomputes the whole deal income split and traces rewards to payers instead. Reserve composition (what the yielding reserves may consist of) is covered by art-06 and art-275; this node takes the reserve value as declared and never re-derives composition.
