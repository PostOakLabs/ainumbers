Deferred Comp 457(b) Comparison

Compare deferring part of your pay into a 457(b) against taking the pay and investing what is left after tax in an ordinary account. See both ending balances, the level after-tax withdrawal over your drawdown, the break-even retirement tax rate, and which path comes out ahead.

Reading the results
This is arithmetic over the figures you enter, not tax advice. Plan rules vary by employer and the plan document governs. The page holds your rates and growth assumptions fixed for the whole horizon, treats the same rate as both the growth rate of the 457(b) and the discount rate for the present-value figures, and models no state rules, no plan-type differences, no required distributions and no early-withdrawal treatment. The 2026 figures it quotes (24,500 standard deferral; 8,000 age 50 catch-up; 11,250 ages 60 to 63; special three-year catch-up up to double the limit, capped here at 49,000) come from the Apex page text and are not checked against an IRS notice, so confirm them each January. The tier shown is the smallest one that would permit your deferral; it does not test whether you qualify. The Apex page note that a governmental 457(b) carries no 10 percent early-withdrawal penalty is UNGROUNDED here and is not relied on. Every input is refused rather than corrected when it is blank or outside its range; a zero deferral is accepted as zero.
