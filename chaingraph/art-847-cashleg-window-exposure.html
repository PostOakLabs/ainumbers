Cash-Leg Window Exposure

When a token leg settles at any hour but the cash leg only moves while a payment rail is open, the principal sits unsettled until the rail next opens. This node measures that wait in seconds, multiplies it by the principal, and prices it at a funding rate you state. You declare the token leg time and either a list of UTC open windows or a rail calendar (weekday hours, UTC offsets by date, holidays). The node carries no calendar of its own and never reads a clock.

🔒 All inputs are processed locally in your browser. No data is transmitted. Do not enter real personal data — use synthetic or anonymised inputs only.
Inputs
Keys: token_leg_time and as_of (UTC, YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ), principal_minor_units (integer string), funding_rate_bp (optional integer basis points), horizon_days (optional, 1 to 60, default 14), cash_instrument (optional: fiat_bank_money, stablecoin, deposit_token, future_instrument), and exactly one of cash_leg_open_windows (a list of open_utc and close_utc) or rail_calendar (rail_id, calendar_version, source_pin, covers_from, covers_to, window_rules, utc_offset_segments, holidays). A window is open from its open instant up to, not including, its close instant. All inputs are caller-asserted.

Basis and declarations

    
This node implements no published standard. It is date and time arithmetic over a calendar you declare. Every figure is a value you asserted; the node cannot tell whether a rail table is current or complete, and it never hardcodes one.

    
Exposure runs from the token leg instant to the next instant the declared cash windows are open. A token leg inside an open window has zero exposure (ATOMIC_POSSIBLE). A later window inside the horizon gives WINDOWED. No window inside the horizon gives NO_WINDOW_IN_HORIZON and no figures. exposure_principal_seconds is the principal times the exposure in seconds, as a decimal string. The optional carry cost is principal times rate times exposure over 10000 times 31536000 (actual over 365), rounded half to even to a whole minor unit. crosses_weekend is true when the exposure interval contains any Saturday or Sunday instant in UTC.

    
Rail calendars: a rail_calendar lists weekday rules (the business-day weekdays, and the local open and close clock times with day offsets, so a rail that opens the evening before its business day is expressible), UTC offsets declared by date range so a daylight-saving change is your statement and not a library lookup, and holidays as business-day dates with no window. Endpoints between local midnight and 02:00 on a clock-change day are not supported; keep rail hours clear of them or use cash_leg_open_windows in UTC.

    
The Fedwire sample declares what the Federal Reserve Financial Services pages state as retrieved on 2026-11-12: the Fedwire Funds Service opens at 9:00 p.m. ET on the preceding calendar day and closes at 7:00 p.m. ET (the customer-transfer cutoff is 6:45 p.m. ET, so declare 18:45 as the close if that is the cutoff you mean), Monday to Friday, closed on the Federal Reserve holidays in the K.8 table. K.8 weekend rules: a holiday on a Saturday leaves the Reserve Banks open the preceding Friday (July 4, 2026 is a Saturday, so Friday July 3 is a business day); a holiday on a Sunday closes all offices the following Monday (July 4, 2027 is a Sunday, so Monday July 5 is closed). The Federal Reserve Board's adopted extension of Fedwire to 22 hours on six days (Sunday to Friday, including weekday holidays) is not yet effective, with implementation in 2028 or 2029; the samples model today's Monday to Friday service. An older Operating Circular page still states a 6:30 p.m. ET close and disagrees with the current schedule; it is not used here.

    
The FedNow sample declares a 24x7x365 service in UTC. FedNow publishes no maintenance window, so none is declared. The sample does not model the roughly 7:01 p.m. ET business-day roll.

    
On the cash leg of tokenized Treasuries: DTCC's own text says distributions and maturities are paid in fiat to Participants' traditional accounts and lists digital cash as a roadmap item for 2027 and beyond; its launch release states no cash leg. The node takes no position on which rail a given cash leg uses. You declare the rail whose hours apply. The 2 a.m. to 6 p.m. ET window DTCC states for token conversion on business days is a different clock from any payment rail and is not modelled.

    
The node never computes a balance-sheet saving or any marketing figure, and it names no unannounced product. Limits: principal up to 18 digits, rate up to 100000 basis points, at most 500 windows and 400 holidays. Every refusal names its code and nothing is coerced: floats, numeric principals, nulls, offsets inside time strings and impossible dates all refuse.
