Operator Exit & Data Portability

You declare your data categories, which components you or your supplier control, your dependencies, escrow arrangements, and your notice / transition terms — this tool checks whether that declared exit posture hangs together. It reports a stranded-category list (categories with no declared export path), an operator-control ratio as declared, single-supplier dependencies with no declared substitute, and whether a contractual-operator claim is supported by the declared component control. It does not audit your supplier, rate a vendor, or opine on contract enforceability — it evaluates the declarations you supply. Undeclared is always kept distinct from a declared failure: silence is never scored as "not portable". Emits a hash-anchored OpenChainGraph v0.4 artifact. Region-portable: the same fields serve DORA exit plans, EBA outsourcing guidelines, FFIEC third-party risk, or any comparable regime — every fact below is an input you declare, never a hardcoded country or scheme.

🔒 All inputs are processed locally in your browser. No data is transmitted. Do not enter real personal data — use synthetic or anonymised inputs only.
⚠ This tool evaluates your DECLARED exit posture only — it does not audit, verify, or characterise any supplier, and no output names one. It does not opine on contract enforceability. It computes once when you press the button, stores nothing, runs on no schedule, and retains nothing.

    What this does not do
    
    
    It does not audit a supplier, rate a vendor, or opine on whether an exit clause is enforceable — those require legal review and evidence this tool never collects. It does not verify that the figures you supply are complete or accurate, and a clean result is not a determination that the underlying contract has been tested. Where you have not declared a fact — escrow, format openness, component control — this tool reports it as undeclared, never as a silent pass or a silent failure.
