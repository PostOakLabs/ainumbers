Goal of this animation

Show seven workflows running in your browser as one database turned inside out, where the pages hold the logic and you keep the receipts. Only a 32-byte hash ever needs to leave your device.

🔒 No personal dataInputs never leave your tab. The only optional upload is a 32-byte hash, and every value shown is synthetic.

How the database is inverted

Usual databaseHere
Schema on a serverPublic tool pages. Same bytes for every visitor, versioned and hashed.
Query engineThe page's own kernel, running in your browser tab.
Index and joinsChainGraph: which page feeds which, published as one JSON file.
RowsReceipts. A result plus a hash of what was computed. You keep them.
Commit logA Merkle root over the receipts, signed by independent timestamp authorities.
Change historyThe lineage registry, checkpointed into the public Sigsum log and cosigned by witnesses.
AuditThe Verification Desk replays every layer offline against public evidence.
Your data at restNowhere on our side. Inputs live in tab memory and go when the tab closes.

What you are watching

Each lane is a real ChainGraph workflow and each card is a real tool page from it. Gold same page tags mark one public page serving two workflows with two different sets of private inputs.

Finished chains drop a receipt into the audit-trail aggregator (cry-05). The Merkle batch verifier (cry-04) folds all seven into one root.

The bottom band is the anchor and verify layer. Only the root goes to the anchor service. Timestamp authorities sign it, and an auditor later replays lane two in the Verification Desk. An edited copy fails at the first check and the original passes.

Chains and steps come from chaingraph.json. Two lanes show the first three steps of longer chains. Input values are synthetic. Hashes are real SHA-256 computed in this page over the demo values shown; they show the shape of a receipt and differ from the kernels' canonical execution_hash. The anchor service, the three authorities, the Sigsum log at seasalp.glasklar.is and its lineage checkpoint (leaf 70,299, eleven witnesses, 4 October 2026) are real and described in the anchors explainer. Their part in this run is staged: no token here was actually requested.
