Sukuk Tradability Composition

Checks whether sukuk with a mixed asset portfolio are tradable at a negotiated price, per authority and per period, from the portfolio composition alone. Two authority profiles are pinned: the SC Malaysia Shariah Advisory Council 266th meeting resolutions of 16 March 2023, and the State Bank of Pakistan IFPD Circular 10 of 2024 Appendix A adoption of AAOIFI Shariah Standard No. 59. Outputs are arithmetic and rule checks against the cited texts, not a fatwa, Shariah certification, legal opinion or regulatory approval.

🔒 All inputs are processed locally in your browser. No data is transmitted. Do not enter real personal data — use synthetic or anonymised inputs only.
What it checks

SC Malaysia profile: at least 33 percent of the sukuk proceeds must sit in non-debt investment assets at the time of each issuance; the percentage need not be maintained through the tenure, but a non-debt component must remain at all times. Any non-debt component means the certificates are a portfolio of assets rather than debt, tradable at a negotiated price. If the component disappears, the portfolio is treated as debt and the sale-of-debt principle applies.

SBP Pakistan profile: tangible and like assets must be at least 33 percent of asset value, including ijarah and diminishing musharakah assets with debt created through tawarruq. Assets combined without a commingled entity fall under the sale-of-debt provisions; a commingled entity that is not entirely composed of debt is tradable.

Only these two profiles are pinned. AAOIFI standards 17 and 59 as published, the draft standard 62 (a status note only, since it is not final), IIFA, CBB, DFSA, DSN-MUI and CMA are refused by name rather than guessed.

Try it locally

Paste a policy_parameters object. Amounts are integer minor units. Each period carries its composition and whether it is an issuance date.
