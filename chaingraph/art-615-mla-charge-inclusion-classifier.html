MLA Charge-Inclusion Classifier

The Military Lending Act caps the Military Annual Percentage Rate at 36 percent, and the hard part is rarely the rate arithmetic. It is knowing which charges have to go into the MAPR in the first place, because 32 CFR 232.4(c) pulls in charges that Regulation Z would leave out of a finance charge, while 232.4(d) lets a credit card account exclude a bona fide fee and then names the charges that exclusion cannot reach. This tool looks up one charge type against that paragraph structure and returns the answer, the exact paragraph it comes from, and a plain statement of why.

How This Node Reaches An Answer
1. Is it enumerated?
232.4(c)(1) lists the charges the MAPR must include. Credit insurance, single-premium credit insurance, debt cancellation, debt suspension and credit-related ancillary products sit in (c)(1)(i) and (c)(1)(ii). The finance charge, application fees and participation fees sit in (c)(1)(iii).
2. Can (d) exclude it?
On a credit card account under an open-end plan, 232.4(d)(1) excludes a bona fide fee other than a periodic rate. Then 232.4(d)(2) names the charges that exclusion does not reach, which is why the first five charge types above never change answer.
3. Or is it undecidable here?
One cell cannot be closed from a charge type alone. When the (c)(1)(iii)(B) short-term carve-out is claimed for an application fee, the answer turns on three facts this node does not collect, so it returns a named review flag instead of a guess.
Two Things Worth Reading Before The Result
The $100 figure is not a credit card ceiling

It is easy to read the participation-fee rule as allowing $100 a year on a card. The $100 in 232.4(c)(2)(ii)(B) governs an open-end billing cycle in which there is no balance, and that paragraph then says in its own words that the limit does not apply to a bona fide participation fee imposed under paragraph (d). The example at 232.4(d)(3)(iv) makes the same point from the other side: a $400 participation fee may still be reasonable on a credit card account. So this node reports the figure and the paragraph it lives in. It never compares it to an amount. That comparison, if you need one, belongs to the actuarial recompute node, not here.

A conditional answer is a real answer

Three charge types come back conditional rather than true or false. That is not the tool hedging. The regulation's own worked examples at 232.4(d)(4)(iii) run the same foreign transaction fee twice on a credit card account and include it the second time, on facts that no charge type can express. A closed-set lookup that returned a fixed boolean there would be wrong roughly half the time and would look confident doing it.

What This Node Does Not Do

    
It does not decide whether anyone is a covered borrower, and it assumes you have established that separately. It does not compute a MAPR or compare anything to the 36 percent limit in 232.4(b). It does not decide whether a fee is bona fide or reasonable under 232.4(d)(1) and 232.4(d)(3), because those rest on comparisons against what other creditors charge for the same service. It reads no external source at runtime: the paragraph structure it applies was retrieved once at build time and pinned by digest, and the page makes no network call. A recomputed figure is something for you to check against your own systems, not a determination about a real transaction.
