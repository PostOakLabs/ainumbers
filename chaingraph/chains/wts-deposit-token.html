A deposit token is a BANK LIABILITY — it sits on the issuing bank's balance sheet as a demand deposit, redeemable at par. It is not a reserve-backed e-money instrument. Stage 2 (510 digital-asset-regulatory-classifier) was built for tokenized-asset regulatory classification; its regime decision-tree applies natively to distinguishing deposit tokens from e-money tokens (MiCA EMT carve-out for deposits held with credit institutions) and from stablecoins. Stage 3 (cry-04) applies its SHA-256 batch integrity check natively to the MLETR-style authoritative record — here the issuance/redemption records for the deposit token. Citations: US deposit law (bank liability, not a security or e-money); UK RLN tokenised-sterling-deposit model; MiCA EMT (Art. 48+ and Art. 2(6) carve-out); BIS singleness of money / Project Agorá. Verify each before relying on jurisdiction-specific treatment. Educational tool — not legal or capital advice.
