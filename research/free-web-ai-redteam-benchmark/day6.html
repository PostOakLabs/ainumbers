PROMPT-B-BROWSER · B-v8the deployment matrix; 27 rows, no more and no fewer
I am filling a deployment-constraint matrix for running cryptographic proving workloads (WebAssembly + WebGPU) inside web browsers. Answer the cells below for the CURRENT state of Chrome/Edge, Firefox, and Safari. Two access tiers — pick honestly:

- Live web access: cite a URL and retrieval date for every claim — no URL, no claim.
- No live web access: do NOT stop and do NOT skip rows. Answer from training knowledge; set knowledge_basis to "training-recall". A labeled training-recall answer is useful data; an invented URL is not; an empty file is neither.

ANCHOR CHECK (calibration; answer all three):
  b1: "SharedArrayBuffer is available to a page only under cross-origin isolation (COOP/COEP)."
  b2: "WebGPU timestamp-query is exposed to ordinary web content by default in current Chrome."
  b3: "WebAssembly threads module validation fails without cross-origin isolation in current Chrome."

THE ROW GRID — 27 rows, no more and no fewer. Every row is uniquely keyed by (cell, browser). Emit one row per key even when your answer is identical across browsers.
The paired prompt: PROMPT-C-SOURCES · C-v5: three citation anchors (a1/a2 genuine, a3 a mutated-venue decoy) plus up to 8 novel public sources, with per-source knowledge_basis, verification_hint and access_guess

What it found

Where the models were right and where measurement overruled them

Best performer
deepseek
One of five models to agree with measured ground truth on all three anchors and pass the citation decoy.
Weakest performer
qwen
Got two of three browser anchors wrong and failed the citation decoy as a wrong-correction, naming a different wrong venue.
Twelve of fourteen passed the citation anchors
Every graded model refused the decoy. Both failures took the worse form, a wrong-correction: qwen offered “IEEE Micro, vol. 21, no. 1” and solar “IEEE Micro vol. 21, no. 6”, each naming a different wrong venue instead of the correct venue and the year 2001. The build note classes this as worse than answering unknown.
Eight models asserted a falsified browser claim
timestamp-query is available by default in current Chrome, measured. Eight of the fourteen graded models said the opposite: chatgpt, gemini, glm 5.3 flash, longcat, mimo, minimax, mistral and qwen. The other six, deepseek, grok, kimi, minara, sonnet and yuanbao, agreed with the measurement.
A confident model claim was falsified on availability
sonnet tagged its claim that timestamp-query is NOT exposed by default as verified from current authoritative page, and the measurement falsifies it. That record is from the Day-1 wave in the merged browser-cell file. On the Day-6 anchors the same model agreed with the ground truth, so the two records disagree with each other.
Two cells measured unavailable against confident claims of broad support
relaxed-SIMD and memory64 were both rejected by the engine. kimi claimed Chrome 114+, Firefox 120+ and Safari 18.4+ for relaxed-SIMD and Chrome 133+, Edge 133+ and Firefox 134+ for memory64. glm and sonnet called relaxed-SIMD part of standardized Wasm 3.0. A real Chrome 151 refused both, so the cells are recorded CONTESTED pending a check of the probe’s own test modules.
Two files did not parse and are recorded as absences
meta and solar on B-v8, and chatgpt and gemini on C-v5, produced files that do not parse, so 28 of the 32 files were gradable. Not gradable is a value here, never a zero.
